What was asked for instead.

"I want to be able to morph the whole head, from a flat puddle, up into the head. And i want to be bale to make the head squeeze and move between things."

That is dec.liquid-glass-melt: a vertex melt on the real bust, not a particle field. Neither ask changes topology, so the rig, the authored visemes, the glyphs and the glass all survive, and it runs on WebGL2.

Only the first half is built. The squeeze is the melt driven by the stage colliders, and building it on an unapproved melt would repeat exactly what produced tier 3.

Nothing here ships

Every gated default is still 0, and melt.amount: 0 is an exact identity, not an approximation. bun run eval passes against the unrecalibrated baseline, which is the guard that proves it.

The escalation judgement

The melt lab exists to answer one question, fixed in advance so the answer could not be reverse-engineered from the result.

If the puddle at melt.amount: 1 reads as a squashed head rather than as liquid, specifically if the rim shows facial features instead of a surface-tension edge, mesh displacement has failed and the particle field in todo.liquid-glass-topology-fluid is warranted.

It did not fire.

At amount: 1 the rim is a smooth ellipse with no facial features anywhere on it. No nose, no brow, no jaw. Mesh displacement has not failed, and the particle field is not warranted on this evidence.

It does not read as liquid yet either.

Two reasons, both known, both fixable without leaving mesh displacement: the puddle has no thickness at exactly amount: 1, and the eyeballs and mouth cavity do not melt with the shell. Neither is a statement about the direction. See Known defects.

What held up across the whole sweep

  • No black patch, no inside-out shading, no collapsed silhouette at any amount. The g' guard holds.
  • amount: 0 returns the approved head with no residue.
  • The glyphs stay welded to the surface and flow with it, which is the approved look.

Known defects

Four. All recorded rather than papered over, two of them found by an adversarial review pass. None is fixed in this change, and each says why.

1. The internals do not melt visible in the lab

From about amount 0.6, two eyeballs and the mouth cavity hang in mid-air above the puddle. The occlusion mask was moved so the shell would keep bounding them, but the internals themselves are separate meshes and nothing displaces them.

The eyeball is a node material and could take the map. mouth_interior and eye_trim are authored glTF materials that cannot take a positionNode at all. Tracked as todo.melt-internals.

A visibility gate was deliberately not built: hiding a mesh partway through the sweep is exactly the popping the acceptance forbids.

2. The puddle has no thickness at exactly amount: 1

Every height collapses onto one plane, so the shell's front and back faces coincide and the glass has no volume left to be glass in. The fix is one line, a collapse band rather than a plane, but the map is fixed by dec.liquid-glass-melt, so changing it needs a decision.

3. The Jacobian guard drops a real sign from review

max(g', 1e-4) is a one-sided clamp, but g' goes genuinely negative near the base before mi saturates. Swept numerically at the shipped settings: negative for amount 0.624 to 0.665 at normalised heights 0.000 to 0.055, reaching -0.0328. A thin band at the base shades inside-out for about four per cent of the sweep.

4. The melt normal is attenuated twice from review

meltNormalGate = clamp01(amount) is used as a mix weight against a normal that is already exact for that amount, so the shading lags the geometry through the middle of the sweep. Both endpoints are correct. Both this and #3 are values the decision fixes, so both are written up for a decision rather than changed by an implementer.

Acted on

The review also caught that nothing tested the occlusion mask's ownership move. test/core.test.ts now counts disposals of all three passes across an avatar replacement and an engine teardown, and the tests were mutation-verified to confirm they can fail.
